The problem

Nepal's highways are long, isolated, and mostly unwatched.

No one is watching

Landslide zones, blind hairpins and fog corridors are long and isolated. Manual patrolling can't cover them continuously, day and night.

Reports travel slowly

Even when someone spots a crash, the information reaches the right department late — often passed between several people first.

Every minute of delay compounds Detection within seconds means response within minutes, not hours.

What we detect

Built for the hazards Nepal's highways actually face.

Different events need different signals. Detection is tuned for the specific hazards found on Nepali highways and hill routes.

Road accidents

Collisions, overturns and multi-vehicle pile-ups detected within seconds, even on blind curves.

Landslides

Slope movement and debris dropping onto the carriageway flagged before traffic reaches the zone.

Floods

Water level rise and road submergence tracked continuously on low-lying and riverside routes.

Fires

Smoke and flame signatures detected near forest belts, tunnels, fuel stations and parked vehicles.

Blocked roads

Stalled trucks, fallen trees and broken-down vehicles that choke the carriageway are flagged early.
